# Roadmap: more of what is real

Ideas for `moon` and the engine, the ones that add the most realism for
the work first. Data from public-domain sources only (NASA, USGS, U.S.
government works); physics from published numbers.

## 1. The sky for a real date and place

The sun, the Earth and the moons where they are, not where a row puts
them. A lunar day is 29.5 Earth days: at Hadley the light would crawl
across the Apennines over two weeks, and on Mars it would follow the real
sol and season. From the Moon the Earth in its true phase, and earthshine
lighting the lunar night (now black). Phobos crossing the Martian sky west
to east twice a day while Deimos hardly moves; Jupiter's moons in their
true places seen from Io. Positions from published orbital elements and
series; a star catalogue only once its terms are known to be public
domain.

## 2. Fly the Apollo landing *(first version: from high gate)*

The lunar module's powered descent from high gate, with its published
numbers: the descent engine throttling between 10 and 60 % (and full
thrust) of 45 kN at a specific impulse of 311 s, propellant burnt by the
rocket equation, sixteen 445 N thrusters in four quads for attitude. The
programs the crews flew: P64, the computer steering to the landing point
and showing the angle to look along in the window (redesignation by
clicks); P66, attitude by hand with the computer holding a descent rate
nudged a foot per second at a time. Contact light from the probes,
engine stop, touchdown within 3 m/s down and 1.2 m/s across, or not.
Dust blown off in flat sheets close to the ground; the lander staying
where you put it.

Next: the braking phase from powered descent initiation (15 km up, nearly
orbital speed, with the radar's update), the descent engine heard through
the cabin, an abort to the ascent stage, and the program alarms.

## 3. Survival as physics

The suit's consumables from the Apollo backpack's published limits
(oxygen, cooling water, battery) and a walk-back limit growing as you
walk away from the lander. Ground temperature from sunlight, albedo,
thermal inertia and shadow: under a crater's lip at Hadley near −170 °C,
in the sun beside it over 100 °C. A dose readout on Io.

## 4. A Mars rover with the light-time delay

A Curiosity-like rover at its real 4 cm/s, driven the way it is: commands
arrive after the one-way light time for the date (4 to 24 minutes), so a
drive is planned from the navigation cameras' views, sent, and seen one
round trip later.

## 5. Metre-scale Apollo sites

The landing sites from the orbital camera's 2–5 m terrain models, once
their terms are checked: Camelot, St. George and Shorty as real ground,
and the hardware where it was left (lander, experiment package, flag, the
rover at its last parking place).

## 6. Light as measured

Regolith brightening sharply looking straight away from the sun (the
opposition surge), from a reflectance model fitted to measured
photometry; Mars's sky brightness and colour from how much dust is in the
air, up to a dust storm; Titan's haze.
